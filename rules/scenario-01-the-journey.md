# Scenario 01 — The Journey

> *Everyone is walking the same road. Nobody agrees on how fast.*

The first scenario, and the one to learn on. Two households are on the road with the Star
overhead and gifts they mean to deliver. Neither is trying to destroy the other; both are
trying to be in the right place when the light arrives.

Play this before any other scenario. It uses no rule that is not in The Conversion Table
and The Core Loop.

---

## 1. The board

Any belén display, or any table dressed as one. Mark out a playing area roughly **12H by
8H**. Precision is not required; a common border of the display is fine.

Choose **six terrain features** and lay them in a rough chain from one short edge to the
other, with **the stable at one end**. A feature is anything a model can stand in or on
and both players can point at: the stable, the well, the market, the palm grove, the
bridge, the hillside, the sheepfold.

Adjacent features should be about **2H to 3H** apart. If your display puts them closer,
the game is faster and meaner; if further, slower and more about Move. Both play.

Everything that is not a feature is Open ground, unless the display makes it obviously
Rough or Difficult (Core Loop §2).

## 2. Setting up

1. **Place The Infant** in the stable. It belongs to neither player. It never activates,
   is never removed, and is not a legal target.
2. **Place the Star** on the feature furthest from the stable. On the first round it will
   begin moving toward the stable, and everything else follows from that.
3. **Build warbands** by The Conversion Table §5. Each player fields 5 to 15 models from
   the figures on hand.
4. **Nominate Gifts.** Each player nominates **up to two** of their models as carrying a
   Gift. A nominated model must have the `burden` attribute if the player owns any such
   figure. Mark it with putty under the base.
5. **Deploy.** Players alternate placing one model at a time, within **1H** of their own
   short edge, starting with the player who did *not* place the Star.

## 3. The Star's road

At the start of each round, the Star advances to **one adjacent feature along the chain**,
chosen by **the player currently behind on points**. On a tie, the player who did not
choose last round chooses; on the first round, the player who deployed first.

The Star may be sent back the way it came. It may not stay where it is.

This is the one rule in the scenario that needs stating twice, so: **the player who is
losing decides where the game is fought.** A player who builds an early lead by holding
the light will find the light taken somewhere they are not. That is the intended shape of
the game, and it is why this scenario does not end in a foot-race to the stable.

## 4. Delivering a Gift

A model carrying a Gift that ends its activation in the stable may **set the Gift down**.
Place a marker; the model is no longer carrying it and may act normally from then on.

A Gift on the ground in the stable is **delivered**. It stays delivered. It cannot be
taken, moved, or removed by any rule in this manual — a delivered Gift is a fact, not a
contested objective.

A Gift still being carried is not delivered and scores nothing. If the model carrying it
is removed, place the Gift where the model stood; any model may pick it up by ending an
activation in contact with it.

## 5. Scoring

Score at the end of each round, after the civilian behaviour table (Core Loop §6).

| Source | Kind | Most per round | Awarded to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Holding the illuminated feature | contested | 1 | The player with more models within 2H of the Star |
| The Infant watched | contested | 1 | The player with a model within 3H of The Infant, if only one player has |
| Each Gift delivered | held | 2 | The player who delivered it, every round including the one it arrived |

**Contested** points are back on the table every round: you hold them by standing
somewhere, and you lose them the moment you stop. **Held** points cannot be taken from you
once earned.

The two are deliberately worth the same per round. A player who delivers both Gifts early
has bought a permanent two a round and must now spend the rest of the game unable to
contest anything, against an opponent who can still score two a round and take back the
board. If held points were worth more, the whole game would be the delivery race and the
Star would be scenery.

Nobody scores for the illuminated feature or for The Infant if the players are level on
models in range. Contested is not shared; contested is nothing.

**Nothing scores for removing a model**, in this scenario or any other.

## 6. Ending

The game ends after **six rounds**. The player with the most points wins. A tie is a tie,
and it is a good result — say so before the first round rather than after the sixth.

There is no way to win early, no way to lose early, and no model whose removal decides
anything. A player who has lost every model still on points can win, and a player who has
removed every model on the board can lose.

## 7. What to watch for, the first time you play it

- **Gifts are worth exactly as much as the light.** Two delivered Gifts pay the same each
  round as holding the illuminated feature and watching The Infant together. Delivering
  early is a lead; delivering with everything you own is a slow loss.
- **Being closest to the Star means acting first and standing in the open.** If that never
  felt like a real decision, the features are too far apart.
- **The trailing player choosing the Star's road** should make a runaway lead feel
  impossible to hold. If it did not, say so — that is the measurement this scenario exists
  to produce.
