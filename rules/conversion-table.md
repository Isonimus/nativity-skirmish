# The Conversion Table

> *You do not need to buy an army. You have owned one since childhood; it has simply been
> standing still.*

This section converts a nativity display into playable models. Work through it once, with
the set in front of you, before the first game. It takes about ten minutes for a household
set.

Nothing here asks who a figure depicts. The procedure asks only what the figure visibly
**is** — its silhouette and its pose — because that is the one thing every set has in
common. A set with no kings still plays. A set with eleven shepherds still plays. The
fisherman, whose presence in first-century Bethlehem has never been satisfactorily
explained, also plays.

**Class is not faction.** Two figures may share a class and fight on opposite sides. Class
gives a model its profile; faction gives it a reason.

---

## 1. Establish the figure-height

Pick the most numerous kind of standing adult figure in your set. Its height, base to
head, is **one figure-height — `1 H`** — and it is the only unit of distance this game
uses.

Cut a strip of card as long as that figure is tall. Mark its midpoint. That strip is your
tape measure for the whole game; ranges are written `2H`, `1½H`, and so on.

If your set mixes scales — a large Holy Family among small villagers, as accumulated
family sets usually are — the most numerous figure still sets `H`. It is the choice that
leaves the fewest models playing at a distorted scale.

## 2. Observable attributes

For each figure, note which of these are true. Every one is decided by looking; none
requires knowing the story.

| Attribute | True when the figure… |
|---|---|
| `swaddled` | is a wrapped or reclining infant, well under half `H` |
| `winged` | has wings |
| `quadruped` | is a four-legged animal rather than a person |
| `large` | stands, at the shoulder, at least half `H` — ox, donkey, camel, horse |
| `mounted` | is astride an animal; the figure and its mount are one model |
| `armed` | carries a weapon or wears armour — spear, sword, shield, helmet, greaves |
| `staff` | holds a long stick with no blade — crook, staff, walking pole |
| `kneeling` | is kneeling, seated on the ground, or prostrate |
| `burden` | carries a load — basket, jar, bundle, gift, sheaf, instrument |

A figure may be several of these at once. That is expected, and the next section resolves
it.

## 3. Decision procedure

Read the tests in order. **The first one that matches decides the class.** Stop there;
later tests are not consulted. The final test matches everything, so no figure in any set
can fall through.

Space between attributes means *and*. A leading `!` means *not*. `*` matches any figure.

| # | Test | Class |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `swaddled` | The Infant |
| 2 | `winged` | Herald |
| 3 | `quadruped large` | Beast of Burden |
| 4 | `quadruped` | Herd Animal |
| 5 | `mounted` | Rider |
| 6 | `armed` | Soldier |
| 7 | `staff` | Drover |
| 8 | `kneeling !burden` | Supplicant |
| 9 | `burden` | Bearer |
| 10 | `*` | Townsfolk |

The order carries the judgement, and it is worth stating what it decides:

- **A mounted soldier is a Rider, not a Soldier** (5 before 6). What matters on the table
  is that it moves fast and arrives first; the sword is secondary.
- **A kneeling shepherd with a crook is a Drover** (7 before 8). The crook is a rule; the
  knees are a pose.
- **A kneeling figure offering a gift is a Bearer, not a Supplicant** (test 8 excludes
  `burden`). What it is holding will matter more than how it is standing.

## 4. Profiles

Four stats, and one trait each.

- **Move** — distance per activation, in `H`.
- **Skill** — the D6 target to hit or to act; roll equal or higher.
- **Grit** — hits needed to remove the model.
- **Resolve** — the D6 target to hold when tested; roll equal or higher. Lower is braver.

| Class | Move | Skill | Grit | Resolve | Trait |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Infant | 0 | — | 1 | — | **Unmoving.** Never activates and is never removed. Scores each round no enemy ends within 3H; that round's points go to the threatening player instead if one does. |
| Herald | 3H | 4+ | 2 | 2+ | **Do Not Be Afraid.** Ignores terrain entirely. An enemy ending its activation within 2H must test Resolve. May not attack a Townsfolk, Bearer or Supplicant. |
| Beast of Burden | 2H | 5+ | 3 | 5+ | **Stubborn.** May carry one Bearer's load, or one non-`large` model, at no cost to Move. Tests Resolve to move away from where it started the game. |
| Herd Animal | 2H | 6+ | 1 | 6+ | **Flock.** Must move toward the nearest Herd Animal within 3H, unless a Drover is within 2H. |
| Rider | 3H | 4+ | 2 | 3+ | **Trample.** Moves through models smaller than `large`. If removed, place the rider as a Soldier and the mount as a Beast of Burden. |
| Soldier | 1½H | 3+ | 2 | 3+ | **Discipline.** Re-roll a failed Resolve test while another Soldier is within 3H. |
| Drover | 2H | 5+ | 1 | 4+ | **Crook.** Once per activation, push or pull one Herd Animal within 1H a distance of 1H. May activate a Herd Animal within 2H instead of itself. |
| Supplicant | 1H | 5+ | 1 | 3+ | **Steadfast.** Cannot be made to flee while within 2H of The Infant. |
| Bearer | 1½H | 5+ | 1 | 5+ | **Encumbered.** May drop its load at any point to gain +½H Move for the rest of the game. The dropped load is an objective. |
| Townsfolk | 1½H | 6+ | 1 | 6+ | **Bystander.** If not activated by a player this round, roll on the civilian behaviour table. |

No trait removes a player from the game. A scenario ends when the scenario says it ends,
and is decided on points.

## 5. Building a warband

A legal warband is **5 to 15 models**, drawn from the figures you actually own.

- **At most one Herald.** Every set has one angel and some have six; six is not a game.
- **At most half the warband from any single class**, rounding down. Eleven shepherds is
  a real belén, but not a real warband.
- **At least three classes represented.** If your set cannot manage three, it is too small
  to field two warbands and you play a one-sided scenario from the scenario section.
- **The Infant is never counted** against the warband size, and is never fielded by more
  than one player.

**Missing pieces.** Nothing in this section is required. A set with no armed figure fields
no Soldiers and plays a warband of Drovers, Bearers and Townsfolk, which is a harder and
more interesting game than the one with the Romans in it.

**Duplicates.** Identical figures are separate models with identical profiles. They are
told apart by position and by a dab of removable putty under one base, not by repainting
your grandmother's belén, which this manual does not sanction under any circumstances.
