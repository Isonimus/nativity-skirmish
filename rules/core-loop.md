# The Core Loop

> *The Star moves first. Everyone else is reacting.*

This section is the game. It assumes you have converted your set (see The Conversion
Table) and that both players have a warband and a strip of card `1 H` long.

A game lasts **six rounds** unless the scenario says otherwise. At the end of the sixth,
the player with the most points wins. **There is no elimination victory.** You can remove
every model your opponent owns and still lose, and this happens more often than new
players expect.

---

## 1. The round

Each round runs in four steps, in this order.

1. **The Star advances.** Move it to an adjacent terrain feature, by the rule the scenario
   gives. The feature it now occupies is **illuminated** for this round; the one it left
   goes dark.
2. **Initiative.** The player whose nearest model is closest to the Star activates first.
   Measure in `H`. If it is a tie, the player who activated *second* last round goes
   first; on the first round, the player who did not set up first.
3. **Activation.** Players alternate activating one model each until every model has been
   activated. A player with fewer models still on the table simply activates nothing on
   their spare turns.
4. **End of round.** Score, then recover.

Being closest to the Star means acting first *and* standing in the most contested spot on
the board. That trade is the whole tactical game; nothing else in these rules needs to
make combat interesting.

## 2. Activation

An activated model may **Move** and **Act**, in either order, or do one twice.

**Move** up to its Move stat, in `H`. Terrain costs are set by the display you are playing
on:

- **Open** — road, plaza, bare ground. No cost.
- **Rough** — moss, slope, stable straw, market clutter. Halves the Move for that
  activation.
- **Difficult** — river, wall, roof, anything a figure would not stand on unaided. A model
  may not enter it. A Herald ignores this, and only a Herald.
- **Illuminated** — whichever feature the Star occupies. Its normal cost applies, but a
  model that ends its activation there counts as **exposed** (see §3).

**Act** means Strike, Grab, or use the model's trait.

## 3. Striking — the opposed roll

One roll each. No arithmetic.

1. The attacker rolls a D6 and must equal or beat its **Skill**.
2. If it did, the defender rolls a D6 and must equal or beat its **own Skill** to turn the
   blow aside.
3. The attacker succeeding and the defender failing is **one hit**. Anything else is
   nothing.

A natural 6 always succeeds. A natural 1 always fails. A model removes one Grit per hit
and is taken off the table when its Grit reaches zero.

**A model with no Skill is never a legal target.** It cannot be Struck, Grabbed or
removed. The Infant is the only such model in these rules, and this is why: the game is
decided on points, and a piece that decides the game by being taken off the table would
make every other rule here decoration.

**Advantage and disadvantage** replace modifiers entirely. Roll two dice, keep the better
one on advantage, the worse on disadvantage. They cancel one another out, and never stack
beyond one.

- The attacker has **advantage** when it moved into contact this activation, or when the
  defender is *exposed*.
- The attacker has **disadvantage** when the defender is on higher ground, behind a wall,
  inside a building, or in a palm grove.

**Grab** is the same roll, but a success moves the defender 1H instead of hitting it. It
is how a model is dragged out of cover, off an objective, or into the river's edge, and it
is usually a better idea than Striking.

## 4. Morale

Nothing in this game is removed by morale. Fear degrades what a model can do; it never
deletes it.

**Test Resolve** — roll a D6, equal or beat the model's Resolve — when:

- a friendly model is removed within 2H;
- a Herald ends its activation within 2H;
- the model is the last of its warband within 3H of any friendly model.

A failed test leaves the model **Shaken**: it may Move *or* Act on its next activation,
not both, and its first Move must carry it away from the nearest enemy. A Shaken model
tests again at the end of the round and recovers on a success.

## 5. Scoring

Points are scored at the end of each round, and only then.

- **1 point** for holding the illuminated feature — having more models within 2H of the
  Star than your opponent.
- **1 point** if The Infant ended the round with no enemy within 3H. If an enemy did end
  the round within 3H, that point goes to the enemy player instead.
- Scenario objectives score as the scenario says.

Nothing scores for removing models. Removing a model is a means — it clears a feature, it
breaks a flock, it makes an opponent test Resolve — and never an end.
